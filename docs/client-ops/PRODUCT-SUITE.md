# Black Label Client Operations Suite

The Client Operations Suite packages owned Black Label capabilities into one tenant-scoped operating product. It starts every commercial line on a shared foundation: catalog, installation, workflow, connector, onboarding, run, approval, artifact, completion-receipt, usage, and cost records.

## Portfolio dashboard and individual packages

The Products dashboard is the single portfolio control surface for 30 top-level entries:

- 17 Client Operations catalog units: eight services, seven vertical packs, and two engagement models;
- Black Label Assistance as the eighteenth new offering;
- 12 existing products, including Marketing, whose Email Automation feature remains individually packageable.

One dashboard does not mean one product archive. Each sellable product or packageable child feature has its own package request and evidence history. Lead Database remains visible as retired and merged into Marketing; Ace remains visible as a private reference and is not sellable.

The registry never infers `verified_working` from source files, a fleet packet, or a prior percentage. That state requires current tenant-scoped evidence for every required acceptance suite plus a verified individual package. A failed or blocked required gate remains visible. New production onboarding begins empty; demonstration installations and evidence are created only when demo mode is explicitly enabled.

### Black Label Assistance release gates

Assistance is currently `design_ready`, not `verified_working`. Its release requires four independently recorded acceptance suites and a verified standalone Mac package:

1. Meeting intelligence: calendar discovery, supported-platform join, consent-aware audio capture, transcription, speaker attribution, summaries, action items, and durable history.
2. Computer use: the accessibility-element operator and the separate pixel-coordinate cursor lane are wired into the agent loop with visible pause/stop control and post-action proof.
3. Conversational voice: wake/dictation, spoken replies, interruption, privacy state, and safe recovery work together in the packaged app.
4. Packaging and security: permission onboarding, clean-machine install, secret scan, signing, launch, upgrade, and uninstall checks pass.

Until those records exist, the dashboard shows the missing gates instead of a delivery date or a working claim.

## Shared product contract

Every service must expose:

- a catalog and signed/versioned manifest;
- client-specific installation and onboarding state;
- workflows with run, pause, retry, history, and retry lineage;
- an Approve, Deny, or Hold review inbox;
- connectors with configuration and health state;
- durable idempotency keys and crash-safe execution adapters;
- artifacts, previews, and completion receipts;
- usage, provider cost, and execution reporting;
- permissions and credential onboarding without plaintext credential display.

## Delivery status vocabulary

- **Foundation ready** means owned platform primitives are implemented and reusable.
- **Integration required** means the service foundation exists but a client/provider adapter and credentials are still required.
- **Design ready** means the package contract is defined while a production execution adapter remains to be built.

The catalog reports these states directly. “Started” does not mean an external phone, CRM, publisher, knowledge source, or data system is live until its connector health and go-live receipt prove it.

Portfolio evidence adds four terminal states: **Verified working**, **Not verified**, **Failed**, and the non-sellable **Retired/merged** or **Private reference** states. Catalog readiness and verification evidence remain separate so the interface cannot turn planned or reusable code into a false live claim.
